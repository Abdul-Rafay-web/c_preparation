§03  FIELD INTELLIGENCE: WHAT CERN IS BUILDING NOW
==============================================================================


01  Triggers are the hardest real time problem
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  • Bunches collide at 40 MHz, producing data on the order of 100 TB per
    second.
  • The Level 1 trigger must decide in microseconds, on FPGAs, whether to keep
    a collision. Anything it drops is lost forever.
  • hls4ml turns neural networks into FPGA firmware, using pruning,
    quantization and knowledge distillation to fit the budget.
  • Next Generation Triggers is a five year CERN project funded by the Eric
    and Wendy Schmidt Fund. Openlab 2025 placed several students directly on
    it, and its public GitHub hosts PQuantML, a compression library
    benchmarked on the same jet data you will use.

02  LLMs are live at CERN
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  • AccGPT is a CERN chatbot pilot built on retrieval augmented generation,
    serving a self hosted Llama 3.1 8B Instruct model with vLLM.
  • Its own paper lists the open problems: retrieval accuracy, larger models
    and fine tuning on domain data.
  • ATLAS has chATLAS, and CERN's ML working group held a full session on
    chatbots in July 2025.
  • Openlab 2024 included an LLM based paper review project for CMS.

03  Software engineers get picked too
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  • A Software Engineering student from Oviedo built a web application for the
    ATLAS Liquid Argon team as a summer student.
  • She then stayed as a technical student, working as a software and data
    engineer on the same calorimeter team.
  • Your FastAPI, React and Figma skills are a real selling point for this
    family of projects.

§06  PROJECT 1: TRIGGERNET  (PILLAR A)
==============================================================================

Make a neural network fast and small enough for an LHC trigger.

WHY IT MATTERS. The Level 1 trigger decides in microseconds, on FPGAs, whether
each collision is kept or lost forever. Networks must be quantized and pruned
until they fit, without losing the physics. This is the frontier NGT is funded
to push, and very few undergraduate applicants have ever touched it.

Levels
~~~~~~
  Level 1 (Everyone), Weeks 2 to 4
    The 16 feature hls4ml LHC jet dataset on OpenML, 5 classes: gluon, quark,
    W, Z, top. Float baseline, quantized version, hls4ml conversion. The
    official tutorial covers this, so on its own it does not set you apart.
  Level 2 (Rare), Weeks 5 to 8
    The constituent level dataset: up to 150 particles per jet, 504,000
    training, 126,000 validation and 240,000 test jets. Deep Sets or a small
    transformer in PyTorch, compressed with PQuantML, swept across bit widths
    and pruning levels, compared against published ultrafast jet
    classification results.
  Level 3 (Elite), February, optional
    Real synthesis reports with AMD Vitis HLS, a large free download. Latency
    and resource tables for your best models.

Pipeline
~~~~~~~~
  Jet data  >  PyTorch model  >  Quantize + prune  >  hls4ml  >  Bit accurate
  check  >  Report + DOI

Build it, step by step
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  01  Load and inspect the data
      Why: Physics data is imbalanced and heavy tailed. Know class balance and
      feature ranges before you train anything.
  02  Train a float baseline
      Why: Report per class ROC curves and background rejection at fixed
      signal efficiency. Trigger people think in 'keep this much signal,
      reject that much background', not accuracy.
  03  Quantization aware training
      Why: The network learns with the low precision it will actually run on,
      so accuracy survives the squeeze.
  04  Pruning
      Why: Weights set to zero need no multipliers on the FPGA, which saves
      real hardware.
  05  Sweep and plot the Pareto front
      Why: Accuracy against bits against model size, in one figure. That plot
      is the whole story.
  06  Convert with hls4ml and verify
      Why: Bit accurate emulation runs on a normal laptop and proves the model
      is deployable, not just small.
  07  Write it up and publish
      Why: A 6 page technical report on Zenodo with a DOI reads like research,
      not a side project.

Stack
~~~~~
  PyTorch, PQuantML, hls4ml, QKeras, NumPy, matplotlib, Kaggle GPU

Deliverables
~~~~~~~~~~~~
  • Reproducible repo with one command runs
  • 6 page technical report with a DOI
  • One figure that tells the story
  • Short blog post

Why it wins
~~~~~~~~~~~
Thousands of applicants can build a chatbot. Almost none can show a jet tagger
compressed for trigger hardware with measured tradeoffs.

§07  PROJECT 2: LUMI  (PILLAR B)
==============================================================================

An evaluated knowledge assistant for particle physics documentation.

WHY IT MATTERS. AccGPT's own paper names retrieval accuracy and domain fine
tuning as open problems. Most applicants will show a chatbot. You will show a
benchmark, numbers and ablations, which is what an engineer at CERN would
actually need before trusting a system.

Levels
~~~~~~
  Level 1 (Everyone), Week 9
    Corpus plus a basic retrieval pipeline and a chat interface. Common,
    necessary, not enough.
  Level 2 (Rare), Weeks 10 to 11
    A published evaluation set of 150 to 200 verified questions, hybrid
    retrieval with a reranker, recall@k, MRR and faithfulness, with ablations.
  Level 3 (Elite), Week 11 onward
    A fine tuned embedding model with a measured improvement, and the
    benchmark released for others to reuse.

Pipeline
~~~~~~~~
  Public CERN docs  >  Chunk + metadata  >  BM25 + dense  >  Reranker  >  vLLM
  answer  >  Eval set + metrics

Build it, step by step
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  01  Build the corpus
      Why: CERN Open Data portal docs, ROOT docs, Scikit HEP docs and arXiv
      experimental particle physics abstracts. Public, relevant, and exactly
      what CERN people search every day.
  02  Chunk with metadata
      Why: Keep source, section and date on every chunk. Chunk boundaries
      decide retrieval quality more than the model does.
  03  Run two baselines
      Why: BM25 alone and dense alone. You need a floor before you can prove
      an improvement.
  04  Hybrid retrieval plus reranker
      Why: Physics text mixes exact terms like NanoAOD with concepts. Hybrid
      search catches both.
  05  Build the evaluation set
      Why: 150 to 200 questions with verified answers and source passages.
      Almost nobody builds this, and it is what makes your numbers
      trustworthy.
  06  Serve an open model with vLLM
      Why: Free Kaggle or Colab GPUs are enough. This mirrors AccGPT's self
      hosted design.
  07  Measure and ablate
      Why: Recall@k, MRR and faithfulness. Then fine tune the embedding model
      and show the delta in a table.
  08  Release the benchmark
      Why: Publish the eval set on Hugging Face and Zenodo. A reusable
      benchmark is a contribution, not a demo.

Stack
~~~~~
  Python, vLLM, Sentence Transformers, BM25, Reranker, FastAPI, pgvector,
  Docker

Deliverables
~~~~~~~~~~~~
  • Repo with one command evaluation
  • Public benchmark dataset
  • Metrics and ablation table
  • Three minute demo video

Why it wins
~~~~~~~~~~~
It speaks directly to problems CERN has written down, and it proves your
Medexa level RAG skills with scientific rigour.

§08  PROJECT 3: OPEN DATA EXPLORER  (PILLAR C)
==============================================================================

Make real CERN collision data browsable in a web app.

WHY IT MATTERS. Many summer projects are tools for detector teams: dashboards,
data quality views, monitoring. This small project proves Scikit HEP fluency
and your full stack strength together, so you match that whole family of
projects.

Levels
~~~~~~
  Level 1 (Everyone), Week 3
    Reproduce the dimuon invariant mass spectrum from CMS Open Data in a
    notebook. The hello world of particle physics.
  Level 2 (Rare), Week 13
    A FastAPI service returning histograms, and a React front end with cut
    sliders and data quality views.
  Level 3 (Elite), Week 14
    Dockerised, deployed, tested, with a clean architecture diagram in the
    README.

Pipeline
~~~~~~~~
  CMS NanoAOD  >  uproot + awkward  >  hist  >  FastAPI  >  React UI  >
  Docker deploy

Build it, step by step
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  01  Read open data with uproot and awkward
      Why: Collision events are jagged: each event has a different number of
      particles. awkward arrays handle that natively.
  02  Reproduce the dimuon spectrum
      Why: The J/psi and Z peaks appearing in your plot is proof you
      understand what the data means.
  03  Serve histograms from FastAPI
      Why: Compute with hist on the server, send compact JSON. Big data stays
      where it is.
  04  Build the React front end
      Why: Cut sliders, data quality flags and clean charts. Your design eye
      becomes a CERN asset.
  05  Package and deploy
      Why: Docker Compose and a live link. A supervisor can click it in March.

Stack
~~~~~
  uproot, awkward, hist, FastAPI, React, Docker

Deliverables
~~~~~~~~~~~~
  • Live demo link
  • Repo with architecture diagram
  • Screenshot gallery in the README

Why it wins
~~~~~~~~~~~
It is the exact shape of project that turned a Software Engineering summer
student into a CERN technical student.

§09  OPEN SOURCE: MERGED PULL REQUESTS
==============================================================================

A merged PR in a CERN ecosystem repository means someone at CERN has already
read your code and said yes. Goal: Two or three merged PRs by mid January.

Targets
~~~~~~~
  • PQuantML. NGT's compression library. Contributing here puts your name in
    front of the trigger ML people directly.
  • hls4ml. The standard tool for ML on FPGAs in particle physics.
  • uproot and awkward. The Scikit HEP backbone for reading and handling
    collision data in Python.

The contribution ladder
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  Rung 1: Read CONTRIBUTING, set up the dev environment, run the test suite
          locally.
  Rung 2: Fix documentation: typos, unclear steps, broken examples.
  Rung 3: Add an example notebook. Your TriggerNet sweep is a natural fit for
          PQuantML.
  Rung 4: Fix a real bug from the issue tracker.

Etiquette
~~~~~~~~~
Comment on an issue before starting it, keep PRs small, respond quickly to
review, and stay patient. Maintainers remember good contributors.

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  • Choose someone who knows your work well. You will not see what they write.
  • Give them a one page brief: your projects, your numbers, why CERN.
  • The letter must be dated within 12 months of applying. Ask for letterhead
    and a signature.
  • For openlab the referee does not have to be a professor, only someone who
    has worked with you. For the Summer Student Programme, get an academic
    referee.

Submission rules
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  • Attach every document before submitting. You cannot add anything
    afterwards.
  • Unprotected PDFs, clearly named: CV, Academic transcript, Reference
    letter, Motivation letter.
  • Rate your English and French honestly. Never claim a skill you only plan
    to learn. Supervisors notice.
  • Submit about a week before the deadline. Portals slow down in the final
    days.


==============================================================================
§13  OPERATING SYSTEM
==============================================================================

About 20 focused hours a week, built around your semester, not against it.

Weekly rhythm
~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  Mon to Fri  2 hours   One hour learning, one hour building.
  Saturday    5 hours   Deep build block.
  Sunday      5 hours   Build, publish, weekly review.
  Exam weeks  8 hours   Maintenance only.

Every Sunday: push something public, update your learning log, and write one
line: what I proved this week.

Non negotiables
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  • Finish TriggerNet Level 2, even if something else shrinks.
  • One measured, documented project beats five demos.
  • Never claim what you have not done.
  • Push something public every Sunday.

Plan B, which also boosts Plan A
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  • Google Summer of Code with CERN HSF or ML4SCI: remote, CERN mentored,
    built on exactly these skills.
  • If 2027 says no, reapply for 2028 while still enrolled. Openlab also
    accepts students who graduate during that summer.
  • Either way, you finish with a trigger ML project, an evaluated RAG
    benchmark and merged CERN ecosystem PRs. That profile opens doors well
    beyond Geneva.

SELECTION IS NEVER GUARANTEED. BEING THE OBVIOUS CHOICE IS SOMETHING YOU CAN
BUILD.
